# Look-Through Earnings

## Definition

The earnings *attributable* to a partial-equity stake — the holder's pro-rata share of the underlying business's earnings — as distinct from the dividend income that actually shows up in the holder's reported P&L. The gap between the two is not an accounting problem; it is a feature. When the underlying company retains earnings and deploys them well, the holder benefits even without receiving a check.

## The retained-earnings corollary

The 1978 letter adds the most important extension: retained earnings at a well-run company may be worth *more than 100 cents on the dollar* to the minority holder.

"Why should we feel differently about retention of earnings by companies in which we hold small equity interests, but where the record indicates even better prospects for profitable employment of capital?"

The inverse is equally important: in businesses with low capital requirements, or where management has a poor record of capital allocation, earnings *should* be returned — via dividends, buybacks, or both.

## How it's discussed in sources

- [[berkshire-1977-letter]] — introduced via Capital Cities: $10.9M invested; ~$1.3M attributable earnings; only $40,000 cash dividend in reported operating earnings. Buffett notes the gap explicitly as a structural feature of partial ownership.
- [[berkshire-1978-letter]] — deepened via SAFECO: $6.1M in attributable 1978 earnings; only ~18% distributed as dividends. "We believe the balance, although not reportable, to be just as real in terms of eventual benefit to us as the amount distributed." The framework shifts from observation to investment thesis — Berkshire's unreported earnings from partial holdings are described as accumulating to "quite substantial" aggregate levels with "equal long-term significance" to reported operating earnings.
