# Social Inflation

## Definition

The phenomenon, named explicitly in the 1977 letter, of a "broadening definition by society and juries of what is covered by insurance policies." Distinct from monetary inflation (which raises the cost of repairing humans and property), social inflation raises insurers' claims costs through changes in legal interpretation, jury awards, and social expectations of coverage.

## Why it matters

For an insurer, the combined drag of monetary plus social inflation sets the **floor** on premium-rate growth needed to maintain underwriting profitability. Buffett's 1977 estimate: costs in Berkshire's insurance lines were rising "close to 1% per month." Unless rates rise at a comparable pace, underwriting profits shrink. This is also the link to the underwriting-cycle dynamic: rate increases lag cost increases, producing windows of profit and windows of pain.
