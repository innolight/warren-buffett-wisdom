# Investor's Misery Index

## Definition

The combined drag that erodes a nominal investment return into a real one. Buffett's formulation:

> **Misery Index = Inflation Rate + Tax Cost of Converting Nominal Returns to Cash**

"Tax cost" means: ordinary income tax on dividends, plus capital gains tax on retained earnings when eventually realized. When the misery index exceeds the business's return on equity, the investor's real purchasing power shrinks — even if they consume nothing at all.

## Why it matters

Most investors evaluate performance in nominal terms. The misery index forces the honest question: after inflation and taxes, is this business actually building real wealth?

The 1979 letter provides the arithmetic. At 20% compounded ROE and 14% inflation, after-tax purchasing power gain approaches zero — because roughly 6 percentage points of the nominal 20% go to taxes, leaving 14% to fight a 14% inflation rate. The result: real return of approximately zero, for extraordinary nominal performance.

Buffett's blunt summary: "A business earning 20% on capital can produce a negative real return for its owners under inflationary conditions not much more severe than presently prevail."

The gold comparison crystallizes this. Berkshire's book value per share in late 1964 bought roughly half an ounce of gold. By yearend 1979, after fifteen years of compounding at 20.5% annually — "blood, sweat and tears" — the book value produced could still buy roughly half an ounce of gold. All the nominal growth was consumed by inflation.

## Implications

1. **High inflation reframes what counts as a "good" return.** A business earning 15% ROE in a 12% inflation environment may destroy real wealth more reliably than it creates it.
2. **The tax layer makes nominal performance even more deceptive.** Dividends are taxed at ordinary income rates. Retained earnings, even if never distributed, create a tax liability on eventual realization. The investor is taxed on the inflation-component of nominal gains — a pure confiscation of purchasing power.
3. **No corporate solution exists.** Buffett is explicit: "We have no corporate solution to this problem; high inflation rates will not help us earn higher rates of return on equity." The misery index is an external condition management cannot control.
