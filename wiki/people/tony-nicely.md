# Tony Nicely

CEO of [geico](../entities/geico.md) through its post-acquisition growth era. Joined GEICO in the early 1960s (34 years of tenure as of the 1995 Berkshire acquisition; 36 by 1997). Named lead operating manager when Berkshire took 100% ownership in early 1996. Oversaw the fastest sustained policy growth in the company's modern history and is among the most consistently praised operators in Berkshire's letters from this era.

## Views and contributions

**The virtuous circle.** Nicely's governing insight is that GEICO's low-cost structure enables a self-reinforcing cycle: lower costs allow lower prices, which attract and retain more policyholders, who generate referrals (over one million per year by 1996, more than half of all new business), which lowers customer acquisition cost, which reduces costs further. The strategic aim, articulated in [berkshire-1996-letter](../sources/berkshire-1996-letter.md), is deliberately *not* to widen profit margins but to enlarge the price advantage offered to customers — compounding the [moat](../concepts/moat.md) rather than extracting from it. "The economies of scale we enjoy should allow us to maintain or even widen the protective moat surrounding our economic castle."

**Two-variable compensation design.** Immediately after the 1996 acquisition, Nicely implemented a compensation plan with exactly two variables for executives and associates alike: (1) voluntary policy growth and (2) seasoned underwriting profitability (policies more than one year old). Critically, first-year acquisition costs are excluded from the profitability measure, so associates are not penalized for writing new business that is initially unprofitable but durable. The rationale: Berkshire will "foot the bill" for new business costs, signaling a structural commitment to long-duration customer relationships over near-term margins. Buffett cites this design as the cleanest expression of [incentive-compensation](../concepts/incentive-compensation.md) aligned to long-term economic value.

**Candid post-mortem.** The 2000 advertising build-up overran its returns — new-policy conversion declined for the first time since the 1996 acquisition. Nicely provided a candid four-factor analysis: (1) diminishing returns on ad frequency once saturation set in, (2) the most responsive direct-purchaser converts already recruited in prior years, (3) stricter underwriting and rate increases reduced price appeal, and (4) State Farm's willingness to absorb enormous losses distorted industry pricing. The frank diagnosis was offered alongside Buffett's endorsement that GEICO's structural cost advantage remained entirely intact — the stumble was campaign-level, not moat-level.

## Notable quotes (Buffett on Nicely)

> In business, I look for economic castles protected by unbreachable "moats." Thanks to Tony and his management team, GEICO's moat widened in 1995. — [berkshire-1995-letter](../sources/berkshire-1995-letter.md)

> At Berkshire we feel that telling outstanding CEOs, such as Tony, how to run their companies would be the height of foolishness … they are the Mark McGwires of the business world and need no advice from us as to how to hold the bat or when to swing. — [berkshire-1998-letter](../sources/berkshire-1998-letter.md)

> Combine a great idea with a great manager and you're certain to obtain a great result. That mix is alive and well at GEICO. — [berkshire-1998-letter](../sources/berkshire-1998-letter.md)

> To steal a line from a competitor, we are in good hands with Tony. — [berkshire-2000-letter](../sources/berkshire-2000-letter.md)

> There is no better manager than Tony, who brings his combination of brilliance, dedication and soundness to the job. (The latter quality is essential to sustained success. As Charlie says, it's great to have a manager with a 160 IQ – unless he thinks it's 180.) — [berkshire-2016-letter](../sources/berkshire-2016-letter.md)

**2006: the 47% productivity milestone.** Between yearend 2003 and 2006, GEICO's policies grew from 5.7 million to 8.1 million (42%) while employees fell 3.5% — a 47% productivity gain. Advertising spend reached $631M, more than any competitor. Underwriting profit: $1,314M; float: $7.171B. Nicely joined GEICO at age 18 in 1961 — 45 years of tenure by 2006. Buffett relayed director Don Keough's reaction: "Forget births. Tell the shareholders to immediately change the names of their present children to Tony or Antoinette." Keough signed his letter "Tony." ([berkshire-2006-letter](../sources/berkshire-2006-letter.md))

## Career conclusion

On June 30, 2018, Nicely retired as CEO and handed the position to **Bill Roberts**, his long-time partner. Buffett's assessment of the succession: "I've known and watched Bill operate for several decades, and once again Tony made the right move." Nicely remains Chairman — described as incapable of doing less than full engagement. ([berkshire-2018-letter](../sources/berkshire-2018-letter.md))

**Buffett's final tribute:** "By my estimate, Tony's management of GEICO has increased Berkshire's intrinsic value by more than $50 billion. On top of that, he is a model for everything a manager should be, helping his 40,000 associates to identify and polish abilities they didn't realize they possessed. All Berkshire shareholders owe Tony their thanks. I head the list."

**Cumulative scorecard since the 1995 acquisition:**
- Pre-tax underwriting profits: $15.5B
- Float: $2.5B → $22.1B
- Sales growth: 1,200%
- Market position: America's #2 auto insurer
