# Al Ueltschi

Founder and CEO of FlightSafety International. Born around 1917, Ueltschi had a lifelong association with aviation that spanned barnstorming in the 1930s, working for Pan Am under Juan Trippe, founding FlightSafety in 1951, and remaining at the helm after selling the company to [berkshire-hathaway](../entities/berkshire-hathaway.md) in 1996 at age 79.

## Views and contributions

Ueltschi built FlightSafety into the world's leading pilot-training company over 45 years — a compound of patient capital, capital intensity, and operator focus unusual in Buffett's portfolio. When Berkshire acquired FlightSafety for approximately $1.5 billion in late 1996, the deal was the company's first major capital-intensive acquisition. Simulators alone cost up to $19 million each.

Buffett's assessment on meeting Ueltschi in September 1996 was immediate: "in about 60 seconds I knew that Al was exactly our kind of manager." That speed of conviction, rare in Buffett's letters, marks Ueltschi as a canonical example of the Berkshire founder-operator ideal — someone whose entire career was the business, and who sought a permanent home rather than a sale.

The deal structure itself reflects how highly Berkshire valued his continued involvement. Ueltschi sought "a home for his company and a security in payment that he would feel comfortable owning throughout his lifetime" — the classic motivation Buffett courts in founder acquisitions. Post-sale, Ueltschi stayed on to run operations unchanged: "We never fool with success," Buffett wrote.

Buffett also invoked Ueltschi in the context of Berkshire's practice of retaining older managers: "It's difficult to teach a new dog old tricks. The many Berkshire managers who are past 70 hit home runs today at the same pace that long ago gave them reputations as young slugging sensations." Ueltschi — described as looking and acting "about 55" at age 79 — was offered a standing joke: Berkshire would split his age 2-for-1 when he turned 100.

By 1997, FlightSafety's first full year under Berkshire ownership produced $139.5 million in operating earnings, confirming the acquisition.
