# David L. Dodd

Co-author with [[ben-graham]] of *Security Analysis* (1934, the founding text of value investing). Long-time Columbia Business School professor. Buffett's second teacher in the Graham-Dodd lineage, alongside Graham himself. Died in 1988; the 1988 Berkshire letter contains the obituary tribute.

## Why he matters

The 1988 obituary is the canonical Buffett tribute to Dodd. Several threads:

> Dave Dodd, my friend and teacher for 38 years, died last year at age 93. Most of you don't know of him. Yet any long-time shareholder of Berkshire is appreciably wealthier because of the indirect influence he had upon our company. ([[berkshire-1988-letter]])

The "indirect influence" is the key framing: Dodd's contribution to Berkshire flows through Buffett's framework — every page of Buffett's reasoning about intrinsic value, margin of safety, and the difference between price and value carries Dodd's imprint via the Columbia education and the *Security Analysis* text. See [[margin-of-safety]] and [[intrinsic-value-vs-book-value]].

Buffett's tribute distinguishes Dodd from Graham on two points:

1. **Personal involvement.** "From the moment I arrived at Columbia, Dave personally encouraged and educated me; one influence was as important as the other. Everything he taught me, directly or through his book, made sense. Later, through dozens of letters, he continued my education right up until his death."

2. **Practitioner success.** "It's particularly impressive that Dave could practice as well as preach. Just as Keynes became wealthy by applying his academic ideas to a very small purse, so, too, did Dave. Indeed, his financial performance far outshone that of Keynes, who began as a market-timer (leaning on business and credit-cycle theory) and converted, after much thought, to value investing. Dave was right from the start."

The distinction matters because the academic-finance critique of value investing has historically been that its practitioners couldn't actually do it. Dodd is one of the cleanest counter-examples — a teacher who also compounded his own capital at high rates by applying the same principles he taught.

## On teaching

The 1988 tribute's distillation of what made Dodd effective in the classroom:

> When students left Dave's classroom, they were equipped to invest intelligently for a lifetime because the principles he taught were simple, sound, useful, and enduring. Though these may appear to be unremarkable virtues, the teaching of principles embodying them has been rare.
>
> I have known many professors of finance and investments but I have never seen any, except for Ben Graham, who was the match of Dave. The proof of his talent is the record of his students: No other teacher of investments has sent forth so many who have achieved unusual success.

## Notable quotes (Buffett on Dodd)

> In Berkshire's investments, Charlie and I have employed the principles taught by Dave and Ben Graham. Our prosperity is the fruit of their intellectual tree. — [[berkshire-1988-letter]]
