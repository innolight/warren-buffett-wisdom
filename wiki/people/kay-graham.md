# Katharine ("Kay") Graham

CEO and Chairman of [[washington-post]]. Buffett's relationship with her began with Berkshire's 1973 purchase of WaPo shares; deepened through his service on the WaPo board and through Graham's increasing trust of Buffett as a counselor on capital allocation. Retired as CEO in 1991 and as Chairman in 1993.

## Why she matters

Two contributions to the Berkshire story:

1. **The repurchase-and-restraint capital-allocation discipline.** Graham's willingness — and Buffett's persistence in advising — to use WaPo's cash flow on repurchases rather than acquisitions at toppy prices is what produced the dramatic per-share value compounding of the 1973-1985 period. The 1985 letter's "triple-dip" framing (business value up + repurchases compounding per-share value + narrowing market discount) credits Graham explicitly:

   > Most of [WaPo's outperformance vs. peers] is attributable to the superb job that Kay Graham has done in the operating and capital allocation areas. Her singular accomplishment has been to manage the company in a manner that has been right, even when "fashion" within the industry was demanding very different behavior. (paraphrase from [[berkshire-1985-letter]])

2. **The dual-class-stock-trust template.** WaPo has two classes of stock with disproportionate voting rights — a structure that other companies have used to abuse minority holders. The 1993 letter is explicit:

   > In 1973, we purchased our stock in [WaPo] for about $10 million. … At the time of our purchase, we knew that the economic prospects of the company were good. But equally important, Charlie and I concluded that Kay would prove to be an outstanding manager and would treat all shareholders honorably. That latter consideration was particularly important because The Washington Post Company has two classes of stock, a structure that we've seen some managers abuse. ([[berkshire-1993-letter]])

Graham is the case study of a controlling shareholder *not* abusing the dual-class structure — and Buffett's repeated emphasis on this point is itself part of the Berkshire framework about [[corporate-governance-modes]] in companies with controlling shareholders (see [[berkshire-1993-letter]] essay).

## Retirement and recognition

The 1993 letter records Graham's retirement and her election to *Fortune*'s Business Hall of Fame:

> Kay's skills as a manager were underscored this past year when she was elected by Fortune's Board of Editors to the Business Hall of Fame. On behalf of our shareholders, Charlie and I had long ago put her in Berkshire's Hall of Fame. ([[berkshire-1993-letter]])

## What Graham operationally did

Drawing on the 1985 and 1988 letters:

- **Held WaPo's capital structure conservatively.** Both WaPo and [[capital-cities]] entered the late-1980s media-leverage era essentially debt-free. The shrinkage in media valuations after 1990 (see [[franchise-vs-business]]) was therefore not accentuated by leverage at either company.
- **Did not chase the late-1980s media-acquisition mania.** Per the 1991 letter: "these companies stayed on the sidelines during the late 1980's period in which purchasers of media properties regularly paid irrational prices." Restraint as a structural feature.
- **Repurchased systematically.** WaPo's per-share intrinsic value rose materially faster than its total enterprise value because of disciplined buybacks at sub-intrinsic prices throughout Graham's tenure.
