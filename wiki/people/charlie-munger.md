# Charlie Munger

Vice Chairman of [[berkshire-hathaway]] and Chairman of [[blue-chip-stamps]] (until the 1983 merger), then Chairman of [[wesco-financial]] (from late 1983, succeeding Louie Vincenti). [[warren-buffett]]'s partner in managing Berkshire's controlled companies. Operates from Los Angeles; Buffett operates from Omaha — distance, Buffett notes, "impedes us not at all."

## Views and contributions

Munger surfaces in the letters between 1980 and 1984 not as a guest contributor but as the visible second voice of Berkshire's intellectual framework. The 1981 letter contains the first explicit credit: "The designated-contributions idea, along with many other ideas that have turned out well for us, was conceived by Charlie Munger." By 1983 he is identified as co-author of the 13 owner-orientation principles ([[owner-orientation]]).

Two recurring themes attach to him specifically in these letters:

- **Partnership-attitude framing.** Buffett's signature "Charlie and I" formulation appears at moments of decisive judgment — buying WPPSS bonds, concentration policy, the GEICO situation. The 1984 letter is explicit: "With 47% of Berkshire's stock, Charlie and I don't worry about being fired, and we receive our rewards as owners, not managers."
- **The Goodwill / economic-moat shift.** Buffett's 1983 admission of evolving away from Graham's tangible-asset bias coincides with Munger's emergence as a visible partner. The implicit credit, threaded through the letters, is that Munger pushed Buffett toward businesses with enduring economic Goodwill and minimal tangible capital. See [[economic-goodwill]].

## Notable quotes
