# Rose Blumkin ("Mrs. B")

Russian-born American founder of [nebraska-furniture-mart](../entities/nebraska-furniture-mart.md). Emigrated to the United States in 1917 at age 23, talking her way past a border guard with no formal education and no English. Founded NFM in Omaha with $500 of savings in 1937. Sold 90% of the business to Berkshire in 1983 at age 90 — and remained on the sales floor seven days a week, "from opening to close."

## Why she matters

The canonical Berkshire **operator-as-shaping-figure**: a founder whose discipline *is* the moat. Buffett's appraisal in the 1983 letter ("I'd rather wrestle grizzlies than compete with Mrs. B and her progeny") is not hyperbole but a structural description — NFM's expense ratios (16.5% vs. Levitz's 35.6%) and gross margins (roughly half of competitors) are downstream of habits of mind that Mrs. B embedded in the family.

The four habits Buffett distills in the 1984 letter:

1. Apply with energy and enthusiasm "that would make Ben Franklin and Horatio Alger look like dropouts."
2. Define area of special competence with extraordinary realism, and act decisively within it.
3. Ignore enticing propositions outside that area.
4. Treat counterparties with high-grade integrity.

Mrs. B's own compressed version: **"Sell cheap and tell the truth."**

The 1984 NFM acquisition is itself an illustration: no audit, no inventory, no title check — Berkshire wrote a $55M check on her word, and Mrs. B's word was the deliverable. The integrity is not adjacent to the business model; it's the same discipline.
