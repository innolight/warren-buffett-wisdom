# Berkshire 2009 Annual Letter

Buffett's letter for fiscal year 2009, dated February 26, 2010. The year's defining event is the acquisition of Burlington Northern Santa Fe (BNSF) — the largest in Berkshire's history, bringing 65,000 new shareholders to the roughly 500,000 already on the books. The letter doubles as a "freshman orientation" for this expanded shareholder base: a structured review of how Berkshire measures itself, what it won't do, and how each operating segment works.

## Key themes

- **"What We Don't Do" (Inversion framework).** Four operating principles stated through the Jacobi/Munger inversion lens: avoid unforecastable businesses; never depend on the kindness of strangers; let subsidiaries operate autonomously; don't woo Wall Street. The cleanest articulation of Berkshire's principles as a set of avoidances. See [inversion](../concepts/inversion.md).
- **BNSF and the "social compact."** Railroads and regulated utilities framed as a two-sided social contract — essential services earn allowed returns in exchange for massive, permanent capital commitment. BNSF will be grouped with [midamerican-energy](../entities/midamerican-energy.md) in future financial statements. See [bnsf](../entities/bnsf.md).
- **Liquidity fortress vindicated.** Berkshire deployed $15.5 billion into the 2008 crisis as a *supplier* of capital while others were supplicants. "We pay a steep price to maintain our premier financial strength. But we sleep well." See [liquidity-fortress](../concepts/liquidity-fortress.md).
- **Stock-as-acquisition-currency deepened.** The BNSF deal required ~6.1% share issuance — accepted reluctantly given the $22B of cash deployed. A structural fix is proposed for the first time: hire a second advisor whose fee is contingent on the deal *not* closing. See [stock-as-acquisition-currency](../concepts/stock-as-acquisition-currency.md).
- **NetJets crisis and Sokol turnaround.** Eleven years of ownership, $157M aggregate pre-tax loss, debt rising from $102M to $1.9B. Dave Sokol arrives in August 2009; by yearend debt is $1.4B and the company is "solidly profitable." See [netjets](../entities/netjets.md) and [dave-sokol](../people/dave-sokol.md).
- **GEICO credit card fiasco.** Buffett overruled his own managers to launch a GEICO credit card — and lost ~$50M ($6.3M operating losses plus $44M on the sale of $98M receivables at 55¢). "I was just older."

## Notable quotes

> All I want to know is where I'm going to die, so I'll never go there. — Charlie Munger (citing Jacobi's "invert, always invert")

> When the financial system went into cardiac arrest in September 2008, Berkshire was a supplier of liquidity and capital to the system, not a supplicant.

> We pay a steep price to maintain our premier financial strength. The $20 billion-plus of cash-equivalent assets that we customarily hold is earning a pittance at present. But we sleep well.

> When it's raining gold, reach for a bucket, not a thimble.

> If Charlie, I and Ajit are ever in a sinking boat — and you can only save one of us — swim to Ajit.

> I was just older. [On the GEICO credit card fiasco, after managers warned against it]

> Don't ask the barber whether you need a haircut. [On investment bankers advising on stock-funded acquisitions]

> It is inconceivable that our country will realize anything close to its full economic potential without its possessing first-class electricity and railroad systems. We will do our part to see that they exist.
