# Berkshire Hathaway 2010 Annual Letter

Book value per share grew 13% in 2010 against a 15.1% S&P return. The 46-year compounded rate since current management took over is 20.2%. The headline event is BNSF's first full year as a wholly-owned subsidiary, increasing Berkshire's "normal" earning power by roughly 40% pre-tax. The letter also contains Buffett's most explicit statement of leverage philosophy ("Life and Debt"), the fullest GEICO 60-year history as an intrinsic-value case study, and the sharpest Black-Scholes critique in the entire series.

## Key themes

- BNSF first full year: 40% pre-tax earning-power increase; 500 miles/ton-gallon (3× trucking efficiency); 11% of all U.S. inter-city freight by ton-miles (BNSF is 28% of rail, which moves 42% of total); 6:1 interest coverage in recessionary year
- Float: 8th consecutive underwriting profit; total float $65.8B (up from $17M at Berkshire's 1967 NICO acquisition); cumulative 8-year underwriting gain $17B — structural proof that disciplined underwriting at scale can sustain negative-cost float through full insurance cycles
- GEICO 60-year retrospective as the canonical illustration that intrinsic value far exceeds book value: $1.4B goodwill carried on books vs. ~$14B economic goodwill (97% × $14.3B annual premiums); market share 8.8%
- "Life and Debt" — most explicit leverage philosophy: minimum $10B cash pledge; "credit is like oxygen"; grandfather Ernest's 1939 letter; Lehman vindication ($15.6B deployed in 25 panic days)
- Net income as meaningless at Berkshire; operating earnings + book value changes are the true measures; the manipulation-by-timing-realized-gains critique
- Black-Scholes "wildly inappropriate" for long-dated options; equity put book: $4.2B premiums received, $3.8B settlement value, $6.7B balance-sheet liability; "approximately right vs. precisely wrong"
- NetJets $918M swing to $207M profit under Dave Sokol; 11 years of aggregate $157M pre-tax losses reversed
- MidAmerican wind: 2,909 MW by year-end 2011, most of any regulated electric utility; Iowa rates flat since 1999 acquisition vs. competitor up 70%+
- Todd Combs hired as first additional investment manager; Lou Simpson retires after 31 years at GEICO
