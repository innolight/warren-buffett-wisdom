# Berkshire Hathaway 2018 Annual Letter

The 2018 letter marks two milestones: the formal abandonment of per-share book value as the headline metric (after nearly 30 years) and the introduction of the "Five Groves" framework as Berkshire's new valuation scaffold. Its most durable contributions are: the sharpest critique of adjusted EBITDA in the series (Lincoln's dog-tail analogy; the SBC-as-gift reductio); the book-value farewell with its three structural reasons; the GEICO / Tony Nicely retrospective ($50B of intrinsic value attributed to one manager); the American Tailwind essay anchoring 77 years of S&P compounding against gold and government debt; and the most explicit articulation of Berkshire's full funding stack (equity, float, deferred taxes) as a structural competitive advantage.

## Key themes

- **Book value abandonment** — after nearly 30 years as the opening scorecard, per-share book value is retired; three structural reasons: Berkshire has morphed toward operating businesses, accounting carries them far below value, and repurchases make book value fall while intrinsic value rises; future scorecard: market price
- **Five Groves valuation framework** — non-insurance businesses ($20.8B pre-tax), equity portfolio (~$173B market), controlled partnerships (~$1.3B after-tax), cash fortress ($112B + $20B fixed-income), and insurance float ($122.7B); intrinsic value approximated by summing the first four minus deferred taxes
- **GAAP vs. operating earnings** — new mark-to-market rule forces unrealized equity gains/losses through net income, producing swings of $25.4B (Q4 loss) to $18.5B (Q3 profit); Buffett calls these "wild and capricious"; operating earnings ($24.8B) are the signal
- **Adjusted EBITDA critique** — stock-based compensation is a real expense ("What else could it be — a gift from shareholders?"); restructuring exclusions ignore recurring business reality; acquisition amortization ($1.4B) is the one legitimate add-back; but $8.4B GAAP depreciation actually understates economic cost
- **The American Tailwind** — $114.75 invested in the S&P 500 on March 11, 1942 → $606,811 by January 31, 2019 (5,288-for-1); 1% annual advisory fee cuts the institutional gain from $5.3B to $2.65B over 77 years; gold comparison and national-debt commentary anchor the case for patient equity ownership
- **Float and funding stack** — float at $122.7B; deferred taxes ($50.5B total: $14.7B from unrealized gains, $28.3B from accelerated depreciation) described as interest-free funding; debt used sparingly with the "Russian-roulette equation" as warning
- **Management succession signal** — Ajit Jain over all insurance; Greg Abel over all other operations; Buffett's explicit statement: "Berkshire is now far better managed than when I alone was supervising operations"

## Entities discussed

- [geico](../entities/geico.md) — Tony Nicely retired as CEO June 30, 2018; handed position to Bill Roberts; since 1995 acquisition: $15.5B underwriting profit pre-tax, float grew $2.5B → $22.1B, sales up 1,200%; now America's #2 auto insurer; Buffett attributes >$50B of Berkshire intrinsic value to Nicely's management
- [bnsf](../entities/bnsf.md) — one of the two "towering redwoods"; combined with BHE earned $9.3B pre-tax in 2018, up 6%; record capex year broadly; GAAP depreciation ($8.4B company-wide) understates true maintenance cost
- [midamerican-energy](../entities/midamerican-energy.md) — (BHE) paired with BNSF as the top two non-insurance earners; 90.9% owned; together with BNSF represent the most valuable cluster in grove one
- [berkshire-hathaway](../entities/berkshire-hathaway.md) — $349B equity capital (largest in corporate America); $112B cash/T-bills + $20B fixed income; $172.8B equity portfolio (cost $102.9B); 15 of 16 years underwriting profit; record capex $14.5B (89% in America)
- [american-express](../entities/american-express.md) — 151.6M shares, 17.9% ownership; ownership grew from 12.6% to 17.9% over eight years solely through buybacks without Berkshire spending a dime; 2018 earnings attributable to Berkshire ~$1.2B vs. $1.3B original cost
- [coca-cola](../entities/coca-cola.md) — 400M shares, 9.4% ownership; $624M dividends; retained earnings per Berkshire share ($21M) slightly negative in 2018

## Concepts introduced or discussed

- [intrinsic-value-vs-book-value](../concepts/intrinsic-value-vs-book-value.md) — the most explicit retirement of book value in the series; three structural reasons why it no longer tracks intrinsic value; future proxy shifts to market price
- [float](../concepts/float.md) — $122.7B at yearend; growth table 1970–2018; 15 of 16 years underwriting profit; 16-year cumulative pre-tax gain $27B; described as functioning better than equity when underwriting profitable; float as fifth grove, the engine funding the other four
- [earnings-quality](../concepts/earnings-quality.md) — sharpest critique of adjusted EBITDA in the letter series; Lincoln dog-tail analogy; SBC as real expense; restructuring as recurring; acquisition amortization legitimately excluded; depreciation understating vs. overstating
- [american-tailwind](../concepts/american-tailwind.md) — 77-year S&P compounding essay; $114.75 → $606,811; advisory fee arithmetic (1% shaves $2.65B off $5.3B); gold vs. stocks comparison; bipartisan prosperity argument; named concept introduced here
- [share-repurchases](../concepts/share-repurchases.md) — repurchases below intrinsic value benefit continuing shareholders; price-sensitivity requirement; Berkshire shifts from 120%-of-book threshold to a more flexible policy; connects to corporate reporting philosophy (no monthly earnings, no quarterly target)
- [leverage-discipline](../concepts/leverage-discipline.md) — "Russian-roulette equation": debt usually fine, occasionally fatal; rational people don't risk what they have and need for what they don't have and don't need; cash pledge of ≥$20B at all times
- [owner-earnings](../concepts/owner-earnings.md) — $8.4B GAAP depreciation understates true economic cost; actual maintenance capex higher; $14.5B total capex (growth + maintenance); echoes 1986 framework in a company-wide context
- [corporate-governance](../concepts/corporate-governance.md) — only Fortune 500 company with no monthly earnings or balance sheets; no company-wide budget; no quarterly "number" to hit; "innocent fudge" → full-fledged fraud trajectory
- [investment-friction](../concepts/investment-friction.md) — 1% annual fee cuts 77-year institutional gain from $5.3B to $2.65B; connects to Gotrocks allegory and The Bet

## Notable quotes

> "Our advice? Focus on operating earnings, paying little attention to gains or losses of any variety."

> "What else could it be — a gift from shareholders?"

> "Abraham Lincoln once posed the question: 'If you call a dog's tail a leg, how many legs does it have?' and then answered his own query: 'Four, because calling a tail a leg doesn't make it one.' Abe would have felt lonely on Wall Street."

> "Berkshire is now far better managed than when I alone was supervising operations."

> "At Berkshire it is a religion, Old Testament style."

> "A Russian-roulette equation — usually win, occasionally die — may make financial sense for someone who gets a piece of a company's upside but does not share in its downside. But that strategy would be madness for Berkshire. Rational people don't risk what they have and need for what they don't have and don't need."

> "The magical metal was no match for the American mettle."

> "Charlie and I happily acknowledge that much of Berkshire's success has simply been a product of what I think should be called The American Tailwind. It is beyond arrogance for American businesses or individuals to boast that they have 'done it alone.'"

> "What starts as an 'innocent' fudge in order to not disappoint 'the Street'...can become the first step toward full-fledged fraud."

> "At Berkshire, the whole is greater — considerably greater — than the sum of the parts."
