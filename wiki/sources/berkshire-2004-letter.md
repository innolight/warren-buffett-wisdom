# Berkshire Hathaway 2004 Annual Letter

Letter from Warren Buffett to Berkshire shareholders for fiscal year 2004. Net worth grew $8.3B (10.5% per-share book value), slightly lagging the S&P 500's 10.9% — Buffett's admitted "lackluster" year for capital deployment with $43B in cash equivalents. The letter's deepest investment content is the most complete GEICO history ever published and the "Portrait of a Disciplined Underwriter" essay on National Indemnity.

## Key themes

- **GEICO origin story**: From Leo Goodwin's 1936 founding on $200K (eliminating the agent entirely) through State Farm/Allstate competitive disruption history, the 1970s near-failure (under-reserving → faulty cost data → inadequate pricing), Jack Byrne's rescue, and Tony Nicely's sustained growth to a projected ~6% market share by 2005.
- **NICO cultural model**: National Indemnity's disciplined underwriting explained in operational depth — the no-layoff policy as the specific mechanism to defeat the fear-of-declining-volume that drives bad pricing; the overstaffing discipline; four CEOs since 1940, none have bent.
- **Insurance performance**: Float reaches $46.1B with $1.551B in underwriting profit across all four segments.
- **Corporate governance**: Three questions that matter (right CEO, not overreaching on comp, acquisitions create value); the Matthew 6:21 "treasure" test for director independence; Berkshire's board standard; two post-bubble reforms (CEO-free director meetings, whistleblower line).
- **Lou Simpson**: Definitive 25-year track record (1980–2004): 20.3% annually vs. S&P 13.5%.
- **Derivatives wind-down**: Gen Re Securities reduced from 23,218 contracts at peak to 2,890 at yearend 2004 — "Like Hell, derivative trading is easy to enter but difficult to leave."

## Notable quotes

> Float is wonderful—if it doesn't come at a high price. Its cost is determined by underwriting results.

> It takes real fortitude—embedded deep within a company's culture—to operate as NICO does.

> Like Hell, derivative trading is easy to enter but difficult to leave.

> Investors should remember that excitement and expenses are their enemies. And if they insist on trying to time their participation in equities, they should try to be fearful when others are greedy and greedy only when others are fearful.

> If ten independent variables need to break favorably for a successful result, and each has a 90% probability of success, the likelihood of having a winner is only 35%.

> [On independent directors:] For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also. — Matthew 6:21, cited by Buffett on director independence
