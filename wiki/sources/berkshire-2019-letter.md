# Berkshire Hathaway 2019 Annual Letter

The 2019 letter is anchored by two major intellectual set pieces: a historical account of why retained earnings are the engine of compounding (tracing the insight to Edgar Lawrence Smith and Keynes in 1924), and the richest corporate governance essay in the letter series (focused on board compensation, director selection dynamics, and M&A process failure). On the financial side, float reached $129.4B, and the GAAP earnings figure ($81.4B) versus operating earnings ($24B) produced the starkest illustration yet of the rule-mandated distortion from unrealized investment gains.

## Key themes

- **Retained earnings as compound interest** — Buffett traces the intellectual history to Edgar Lawrence Smith's 1924 book and Keynes' review, establishing that the idea of retained earnings as value was genuinely novel at the time; the 10-company look-through table ($3.8B dividends, $8.3B Berkshire's share of retained earnings) is the most explicit recent quantification
- **GAAP vs. operating earnings** — 2018 GAAP: $4B (unrealized losses); 2019 GAAP: $81.4B (unrealized gains); a "crazy 1,900% increase" driven by accounting, not business performance; operating earnings were "little changed"
- **Corporate governance** — the most extensive board essay in the series: the fee-dependent "NWD" (non-wealthy director) incentive analysis; the cocker-spaniel CEO selection dynamic; M&A advisory process failure; the independence paradox
- **Float and P/C industry rate headwinds** — $129.4B float, 16 of last 17 years profitable; P/C industry compressed from 5-6¢ to 2-3¢ per dollar of float; Berkshire's investment flexibility as a structural advantage
- **BHE 20-year retrospective** — Iowa wind self-sufficiency by 2021; residential rates up <1%/year since 2000 vs. competitor's +70%; $28B retained earnings never paid out as dividends
- **Succession and Berkshire's durability** — Buffett's will directs executors to hold all Berkshire shares; 12-15 years to distribute (A→B conversions); five-factor durability case; Ajit Jain and Greg Abel to receive more exposure

## Notable quotes

> "These studies are the record of a failure – the failure of facts to sustain a preconceived theory." — Edgar Lawrence Smith (1924), discovering retained earnings were the key to stock outperformance

> "Well-managed industrial companies do not, as a rule, distribute to the shareholders the whole of their earned profits. In good years, if not in all years, they retain a part of their profits and put them back into the business. Thus there is an element of compound interest (Keynes' italics) operating in favour of a sound industrial investment. Over a period of years, the real value of the property of a sound industrial is increasing at compound interest, quite apart from the dividends paid out to the shareholders." — John Maynard Keynes, reviewing Smith's book (1924)

> "To achieve a reputation as a good manager, just be sure you buy good businesses." — Tom Murphy, on acquisitions

> "[A]cquisitions are similar to marriage: They start, of course, with a joyful wedding – but then reality tends to diverge from pre-nuptial expectations."

> "Don't ask the barber whether you need a haircut." — on relying on M&A advisors to evaluate M&A deals

> "When seeking directors, CEOs don't look for pit bulls. It's the cocker spaniel that gets taken home."

> "[T]hose market gyrations led to a crazy 1,900% increase in GAAP earnings!"

> "The more we can invest, the more we like it." — on BHE never paying a dividend since the 2000 acquisition
