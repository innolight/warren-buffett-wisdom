# Berkshire Hathaway 2016 Annual Letter

The 2016 letter covers Berkshire's $27.5B gain in net worth (10.7% per-share book value growth) and marks 52 years under present management. Its most durable contributions are: the clearest articulation of the share-repurchases doctrine in the series (three-partner analogy, two exceptions, "What is smart at one price is stupid at another"); the revolving-fund framing of float (the most explicit case for why GAAP overstates the float liability); nine-year results from the index-vs-hedge-fund bet (S&P +85.4% vs. fund-of-funds average ~2.2%); and a pointed critique of "adjusted earnings" that exclude restructuring costs and stock-based compensation.

## Key themes

- **Share repurchases doctrine** — only value-creating below intrinsic value; three-partner analogy makes the arithmetic concrete; two legitimate exceptions; Berkshire's own 120%-of-book threshold explained
- **Float as revolving fund** — the most explicit statement that float should not be treated as a conventional liability; $100B milestone crossed; 14 consecutive years of underwriting profit, $28B pre-tax
- **The Bet conclusion** — nine years of empirical data validating index-fund superiority over hedge funds; Jack Bogle tribute; the Group A/B cost arithmetic as the theoretical foundation
- **Adjusted earnings critique** — systematic exclusion of restructuring costs and SBC misleads shareholders and breeds a culture of number manipulation downstream
- **BNSF depreciation gap** — GAAP depreciation materially understates true economic cost across the railroad industry; maintenance capex far exceeds the accounting charge

## Entities discussed

- [geico](../entities/geico.md) — 12% market share (from 2.5% in 1995 when Berkshire acquired control); Tony Nicely completes 55 years; growth accelerated in H2 2016 as competitors retreated during profit squeeze; annual sales in 2016 equal to $8M every three hours (vs. $8M annual in 1951)
- [bnsf](../entities/bnsf.md) — GAAP depreciation ($2.1B) insufficient to hold competitive position; true maintenance capex substantially higher; revenue 3¢/ton-mile vs. 4–5¢ for major competitors; interest coverage 6:1 in a disappointing year for railroads
- [midamerican-energy](../entities/midamerican-energy.md) — Iowa retail rate 7.1¢/KWH (vs. 9.9¢ Alliant, 10.3¢ national average); base rates locked until 2029; wind generation = 55% of Iowa retail sales, en route to 89% by 2020; BHE + BNSF = 33% of Berkshire's after-tax operating earnings
- [berkshire-hathaway](../entities/berkshire-hathaway.md) — float crossed $100B; $86B cash and equivalents (95% U.S.-domiciled); investments include first Apple position ($6.7B cost, 61M shares)

## Notable quotes

> "What is smart at one price is stupid at another."

> "Owing $1 that in effect will never leave the premises — because new business is almost certain to deliver a substitute — is worlds different from owing $1 that will go out the door tomorrow and not be replaced."

> "If our revolving float is both costless and long-enduring, which I believe it will be, the true value of this liability is dramatically less than the accounting liability."

> "Every decade or so, dark clouds will fill the economic skies, and they will briefly rain gold. When downpours of that sort occur, it's imperative that we rush outdoors carrying washtubs, not teaspoons."

> "Charlie and I cringe when we hear analysts talk admiringly about managements who always 'make the numbers.' In truth, business is too unpredictable for the numbers always to be met."

> "When a person with money meets a person with experience, the one with experience ends up with the money and the one with money leaves with experience."

> "If there were ever to be another Ajit and you could swap me for him, don't hesitate. Make the trade!"

> "[Jack Bogle] is a hero to them and to me."
