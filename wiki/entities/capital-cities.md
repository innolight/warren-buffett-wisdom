# Capital Cities Communications

Media and broadcasting company led by **Tom Murphy** (CEO) and Dan Burke. Berkshire's first position was opened in 1977 ($10.9M cost, $13.2M market). The deeper Berkshire investment came in March 1985 (subsequent event in the 1984 letter): a $172.50 commitment per share for 3 million shares contingent on Cap Cities' acquisition of ABC.

## Why it matters

Two distinct (and interlocking) reasons:

1. **The look-through-earnings exemplar.** The 1977 letter used Cap Cities to illustrate how partial-equity ownership of an excellent business can outperform whole-company control of a mediocre one. Attributable earnings ~$1.3M vs. $40,000 in cash dividends — a textbook gap between accounting and economic reality. See [[look-through-earnings]].

2. **Tom Murphy as Buffett's "twofer" managerial exemplar.** The 1981 letter names Murphy among the rare managers who are both Category-1 acquirers (buying inflation-friendly businesses with pricing power and low capital needs) *and* Category-2 operators (running them well after acquisition). Buffett's framing: "We salute such managers as Ben Heineman at Northwest Industries, Henry Singleton at Teledyne, Erwin Zaban at National Service Industries, and especially Tom Murphy at Capital Cities Communications (a real managerial 'twofer'…)."

The 1984 letter sharpens the appraisal: "Our admiration for the management of Capital Cities, led by Tom Murphy and Dan Burke, has been expressed several times in previous annual reports. Quite simply, they are tops in both ability and integrity."

## On Tom Murphy

(Folded into this page per the wiki's preference against separate operator pages.)

Murphy is the canonical illustration of what Buffett means by a *Category 2* manager: a recognizer of mispriced businesses combined with the operating discipline to compound their value after purchase. Two features Buffett returns to:

- **Disciplined capital allocation.** Murphy's preference, when no compelling acquisition presents itself, is to repurchase Cap Cities shares — "have found repurchase of their own shares to be the most sensible employment of corporate capital" (1981).
- **Integrity as a structural feature.** Buffett's repeated emphasis on Murphy's "ability *and* integrity" is not throwaway praise — it's the trait that lets a manager hold to disciplined capital allocation against institutional pressure.

The combination — discipline + integrity + recognition + operating ability — is rare enough that Buffett's investment thesis on a Cap Cities/ABC combination is in large part a bet on the management team's continued ability to apply it at larger scale.
