# National Indemnity Company

Auto and general liability insurer headquartered in Omaha. Acquired by [[berkshire-hathaway]] in early 1967 together with sister company National Fire and Marine for approximately $8.6M total — the entry point into insurance, and the company around which Berkshire's float-driven model was built.

## History

In 1967, the two sister companies had $22M of combined premium volume. By 1977, Berkshire's aggregate insurance premium volume — National Indemnity plus all subsequent additions — had reached $151M, an almost 600% increase achieved without issuing any Berkshire shares.

Expansion came through three channels: (1) growth in National Indemnity's traditional liability lines; (2) a new homestate insurance group (Cornhusker 1970, Lakeland 1971, Texas United 1972, Iowa 1973, Kansas Fire 1977); (3) cash acquisitions (Home and Automobile 1971, Kerkling Reinsurance / now Central Fire and Casualty 1976, Cypress Insurance yearend 1977).

## Why it matters

National Indemnity is Buffett's canonical case for **underwriting discipline as the cost of float**. The 1977 letter credits chief underwriter Phil Liesche with "an extraordinary underwriting achievement" — large volume gains paired with excellent margins as competitors retreated after the 1974–75 crisis — and uses the company to articulate the discipline of accepting reduced volume in soft markets, an early form of the [[institutional-imperative]] argument.

National Indemnity also illustrates the [[float]] mechanic: insurance reserves plus retained earnings drove Berkshire's investments at cost from $134.6M (1975) to $252.8M (1977), with pre-tax net investment income climbing $8.4M → $12.3M.

## Notable mistakes acknowledged (1977 letter)

- Surety operation initiated 1969
- 1973 expansion of Home & Auto's urban auto marketing into Miami
- An "unresolved aviation fronting arrangement"
- Worker's Comp operation in California (under reorganization)

Buffett's framing: "It is comforting to be in a business where some mistakes can be made and yet a quite satisfactory overall performance can be achieved."

## 1985-1995 — the platform for the super-cat franchise

National Indemnity is also the corporate vehicle through which Berkshire's super-cat reinsurance franchise is written from 1989 onward. Key milestones:

- **1985:** Berkshire's premium volume more than triples; large flight-to-quality from sophisticated insureds. The Fireman's Fund quota-share written through National Indemnity.
- **1986:** Combined ratio improved to 103 from 111. Premium volume now driven by "large risks" ($1-3M+ single policies) and the FFIC contract.
- **1988-89:** Ajit Jain joins; super-cat reinsurance debut post-Hurricane Hugo and California earthquake.
- **1992:** Combined ratio 84.2 in the National Indemnity traditional book (run by Don Wurster). Hurricane Andrew super-cat loss $125M but well-managed.
- **1995:** Three-year traditional-book average combined ratio 85.6. Don Wurster, Rod Eldred, Brad Kinstler, Ajit Jain, John Kizer named as the operating leadership.

The 1995 super-cat examples (illustrating the diversified non-traditional book): Mike Tyson life insurance, Lloyd's mortality coverage on 225 "names," Chinese satellite launch+orbit. See [[super-cat-insurance]].
