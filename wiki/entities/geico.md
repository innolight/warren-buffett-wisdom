# GEICO (Government Employees Insurance Company)

Direct-to-consumer auto insurer. By the 1977 letter, already a material Berkshire marketable-equity holding — purchased during/after the company's near-bankruptcy crisis of the mid-1970s.

## Position as of yearend 1977

| Security | Shares | Cost | Market |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Convertible Preferred | 1,986,953 | $19,417,000 | $33,033,000 |
| Common Stock | 1,294,308 | $4,116,000 | $10,516,000 |

Combined cost $23.5M, market $43.5M — by far the largest equity position in the Berkshire insurance subsidiaries' portfolio at this date.

## Why it matters

GEICO becomes a multi-decade Berkshire story: marketable holding here in 1977, eventually a wholly-owned subsidiary in 1996. The 1977 position represents Buffett's conviction buy on a structurally low-cost auto insurer during its post-near-failure recovery — a textbook application of his [[acquisition-criteria]] to a distressed-but-fixable franchise.
