# The Scott & Fetzer Company

Cleveland-based diversified industrial conglomerate acquired by Berkshire right after year-end 1985 for ~$320M (plus ~$90M of existing debt). Sales ~$700M from 17 businesses, with **World Book** (encyclopedias) accounting for about 40% of sales and a bit more of income. Other lines include **Kirby** home-care systems, **Campbell Hausfeld** air compressors, and **Wayne** burners and water pumps. CEO **Ralph Schey**, in the role since ~1976.

## Why it matters

Scott Fetzer is **the prototype Berkshire acquisition** as Buffett defines the acquisition criteria — and is described as such explicitly:

> Scott Fetzer is a prototype — understandable, large, well-managed, a good earner.

It satisfies every element of the [[acquisition-criteria]] six-point ad (as raised in 1985 to a $10M after-tax earnings minimum). Each business returns good-to-excellent capital. Management in place. No technology problem. Simple businesses.

Two specific durable points:

1. **World Book as a "value franchise."** Sells more sets in the U.S. than its four largest competitors combined, while pricing below every major competitor — a low-price-plus-best-product structural position. Childcraft, a related product, is described in the same terms. The 1985 letter uses this to anchor the bet despite "declining results for many companies in the direct-selling industry."
2. **Ralph Schey as a capital allocator who narrowed the focus.** When Schey took charge, Scott Fetzer was the residue of a 1960s acquisition spree (31 businesses). He divested the misfits but stopped short of indiscriminate pruning — he bought World Book in 1978 when it became available, an opportunistic addition that turned out to be the franchise. Buffett's phrase: "Ralph's operating and capital-allocation record is superb."

## Acquisition history

The deal arrival is one of the letter's clearer illustrations of Buffett's "haphazard approach to acquisitions":

- 1984: Scott Fetzer becomes a public auction candidate via a major investment-banking firm.
- Mid-1985: An ESOP-led sale plan is approved by shareholders but fails at closing.
- October 10, 1985: Buffett — who had been following the saga in newspapers and did not know Schey — writes a short letter introducing himself and asking if Schey might like to talk.
- October 22, 1985: Buffett and Munger meet Schey for dinner in Chicago.
- Following week: acquisition contract signed.
- Right after year-end 1985: deal closes for ~$320M.

> We have no master strategy, no corporate planners delivering us insights about socioeconomic trends, and no staff to investigate a multitude of ideas presented by promoters and intermediaries. Instead, we simply hope that something sensible comes along — and, when it does, we act.

## Financial impact

The Scott Fetzer acquisition, combined with the dramatic growth in Berkshire's insurance volume, was projected to push Berkshire's 1986 revenues above $2B — more than double 1985.

## Operating notes

- 17 businesses, "many leaders in their fields."
- World Book ~40% of sales / slightly more of income.
- World Book regularly rated most useful encyclopedia by teachers, librarians, and consumer guides — yet priced below all major competitors.
- "Return on invested capital is good to excellent for most of these businesses."
